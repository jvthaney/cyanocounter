Who did the counting. Used only to name the downloaded file and to record who to ask about the count later.

The day the sample was taken from the lake, not the day you counted it.

Roughly when the sample was taken. Plankton move up and down through the day, so the time of a tow affects what is in it.

The lake, and the spot in it: a station name, a landmark, or the deep hole. Anything that lets someone repeat the tow.

In decimal degrees. A phone's map app will give you this if you press and hold on your location.

In decimal degrees, negative west of Greenwich, so negative throughout North America.

How much water you ended up with AFTER the tow: the volume in your collection bottle or graduated cylinder, with the plankton from the whole tow concentrated into it. This is NOT the volume of lake water that passed through the net, which is worked out from the tow depth and net diameter instead.

A Sedgewick Rafter cell is 50 columns across and 20 rows down, so 1,000 squares in all. Count a whole slide and enter 1000. For part of one, multiply the full rows you finished by 50 and add the squares in the row you stopped partway through: 5 full rows plus 23 squares is 273.

How deep the net went before you hauled it up. With the net diameter this gives the volume of lake water the tow filtered.

How many separate hauls went into this one sample. If you combined three tows into the same bottle, enter 3.

The width of the mouth of the plankton net, across the metal ring. Measure it once and reuse the figure: a common net is 0.16 m.

How much of that concentrated sample you put on the slide and counted. On a Sedgewick Rafter cell this is 1 ml. Together with the concentrated sample volume it tells us what fraction of the tow you looked at.

optional

OPTIONAL. Leave this blank to count the whole slide. If you would rather stop at a set total, enter it here and a progress bar will track your running count and turn orange when you reach it. Stopping at 300 gives a counting error of about 6 per cent; stopping at 100 about 10 per cent.

Anything worth remembering: weather, a visible bloom, water colour, preservative used, taxa you saw but did not count.
